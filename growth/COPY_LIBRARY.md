## Persona landing sections
**New revert with little ones:** Start with warmth, gratitude, simple duas and parent-led routines.
**School-age children:** Turn big questions into conversations and short family lessons.
**Teenagers:** Listen first. Explore belief, identity and questions without pressure.
**Single parent:** Five-minute activities for busy days and community support when needed.
**Interfaith household:** Practical tools for respectful co-parenting and different traditions.
**Returning Muslim parent:** Refresh your knowledge without shame and start together.

## Email welcome sequence
**Email 1 — Subject: Welcome to learning together**
You don't have to know everything to begin. RevertParenting is being built to help you learn an Islamic concept, explain it to your child and try a simple activity together. Start with one conversation, not a perfect schedule.

**Email 2 — Subject: When your child asks why**
Children's questions can be an invitation to connect. You can say, 'I'm still learning, too. Let's explore this together.' Our upcoming lessons pair parent explanations with age-appropriate conversation prompts.

**Email 3 — Subject: Five minutes can matter**
A shared story, a small act of kindness or a moment of gratitude can become a meaningful family routine. Our curriculum is designed for real homes and flexible schedules.

**Email 4 — Subject: Help shape RevertParenting**
If you embraced Islam while raising children, your experience matters. We're inviting parents and educators to help shape the product. Contribute through our GitHub project or future research opportunities.

## Press description
RevertParenting is an in-development Islamic family learning platform that combines parent education, child-friendly teaching support, and practical family activities. The project aims to support revert parents and diverse Muslim households through verified sources, qualified review and respectful, developmentally appropriate guidance.

## Copy guardrails
Do not promise conversion outcomes, shame families, claim scholarly endorsement without approval, fabricate testimonials, or imply an app replaces parents, teachers or qualified scholars.
