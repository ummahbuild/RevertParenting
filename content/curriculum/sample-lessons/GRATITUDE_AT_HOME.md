# Lesson: Gratitude at Home

Status: DRAFT. Family activity for ages 3–18; caregiver modeling for ages 0–2. Approx. 5–10 minutes.

## Parent primer
Gratitude can be practiced by noticing good things, thanking people and showing kindness. Avoid suggesting that a child must feel happy all the time or that sadness means they are ungrateful. A family can appreciate something while also acknowledging hardship.

## Parent script
'Today I'd like us to notice something good in our day. It's also okay if something was hard. What is one thing you appreciated, and what is one thing you wish had gone differently?'

## Child variants
- 0–2: parent models saying thank you and showing care.
- 3–5: name one helpful person or comforting thing; draw a picture.
- 6–8: make a gratitude card for someone.
- 9–12: discuss how gratitude can lead to helping others.
- 13–15: explore gratitude alongside disappointment or injustice.
- 16–18: reflect on gratitude, entitlement and social responsibility without minimizing hardship.

## Family activity
1. Gather for five minutes.
2. Each person may share one appreciation; passing is allowed.
3. Choose one kind action for tomorrow.
4. Close with optional personal reflection.
5. Parent privately records any questions to revisit.

## Accessibility
Use objects, pictures, sign language, audio or text. No forced public sharing. Offline printable option.
