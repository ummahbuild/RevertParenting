# Lesson: Questions Are Welcome

Status: DRAFT. Age groups 6–18 with guardian guidance. Approx. 10 minutes.

## Parent primer
A parent learning Islam may feel pressure to answer every question immediately. It is more responsible to say when you do not know, check reliable sources and return to the conversation. Your child may be asking for reassurance as much as information. Invite questions without treating them as evidence of poor character or weak faith.

**Evidence research leads:** Qur'an 16:125 (wisdom in communication) and Sahih al-Bukhari 69 (making matters easy). These references must be verified for exact wording, context and appropriate application before publishing. The listening exercises below are practical parenting suggestions, not direct scriptural quotations.

## Suggested parent script
'I'm glad you asked. I don't know everything yet, but I want to learn carefully. What made you think about that question? We can look for a reliable answer together.'

## Child variants
- 0–2: caregivers model warm, responsive communication; no direct lesson.
- 3–5: 'You can ask me questions. Let's find out together.'
- 6–8: invite the child to draw a question and choose one to explore.
- 9–12: make a two-column sheet: what we know / what we want to check.
- 13–15: discuss how to distinguish trustworthy information from a confident opinion.
- 16–18: compare a primary source, translation and named scholarly explanation.

## Family activity
Materials: paper and pencil, optional. Each person writes or says one question. Parent models 'I don't know yet' for one question. Choose a time to research using approved references or ask a qualified teacher. No requirement to disclose private doubts publicly.

## Reflection
What did my child actually want to know? Did I listen before responding? Which source needs checking?

## Safety
Never punish or shame a child for asking. Do not promise certainty on disputed matters. Escalate distress or family conflict to appropriate support.
