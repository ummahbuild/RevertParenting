# Lesson: Telling My Child About My Journey to Islam

Status: DRAFT. Parent-facing family conversation coaching; not a ruling.

## Parent primer
A child may wonder what changed after a parent embraced Islam, whether family routines will change and whether they are still loved. A good first conversation explains the parent's personal journey honestly, reassures the child about the relationship, and makes room for questions and feelings. Families with multiple guardians must respect applicable agreements and laws.

**Evidence research leads:** Qur'an 16:125 on wisdom and Qur'an 17:23 on family kindness. Relevance and interpretation require qualified review; the scripts are practical guidance rather than revealed wording.

## Suggested script
'I've been learning about Islam, and I chose to become Muslim. It is important to me, and I'm still learning. I love you, and you can ask me questions. Some of our routines may change, and we can talk about those changes together.'

## Age adaptations
- 0–2: no formal explanation required; prioritize predictable caregiving.
- 3–5: use a few sentences and reassure the child.
- 6–8: explain one visible routine change and ask what they noticed.
- 9–12: invite questions about school, food, relatives and celebrations.
- 13–15: discuss identity, autonomy, family expectations and differences respectfully.
- 16–18: provide room for independent views and deeper source discussion.

## Family activity
Create a 'Questions for Later' card. Invite questions and choose one to research together. Do not demand immediate agreement or religious identification.

## Difficult situations
If another guardian disagrees, avoid instructing secrecy or undermining lawful parenting agreements. For coercion, abuse or serious conflict, seek appropriate qualified help.
